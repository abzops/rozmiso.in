# Seasonal Demand (Nov 2026 – Jan 2027) and COD Dropship Unit Economics in India

Research date: 30 Oct 2026. Note: most benchmark figures come from vendor/agency blogs (logistics, checkout, ad agencies, calculator sites) with commercial interests; treat as directional. Google Trends could not be queried directly (JS-rendered; no export via fetch), so seasonality is evidenced by press/retailer data instead.

## 1. Which winter products rise in India in Nov–Jan?

### Takeaway
Heating products in India are a short, North-India-driven season (roughly Oct–Jan) whose intensity tracks cold waves; room heaters, geysers and winter wear demand can double in cold snaps. Direct Google Trends curves for hand warmers, heated insoles, lint removers, etc. were not obtainable, so item-level ranking is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Flipkart (Jan 2024): fan and halogen room heaters and storage geysers are the most popular categories in peak winter; one retailer saw a ~2x jump in room-heater demand during a cold wave — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/magazine/the-buzz/story/cold-wave-how-the-ongoing-winter-season-has-led-to-a-spike-in-sale-of-appliances-like-geysers-room-heaters-414197-2024-01-19)
- Flipkart reported heating-appliance demand up ~33% YoY, especially in the North, spreading to tier-2/3 cities — [Business Standard](https://bsppd.business-standard.com/article/companies/demand-for-heating-appliances-up-33-year-on-year-says-flipkart-123011001165_1.html)
- North India is described as ~80% of the room-heater business; the market is concentrated in Oct–Jan — [Business Standard (older)](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/normal-winter-raises-trade-hope-117122701075_1.html); [Indiagri room heater report](https://matrix.indiagri.in/room-heaters-report)
- Room heater market estimate ~US$94.6M (2023), ~6.8% CAGR to 2029 (estimates vary widely) — [Indiagri](https://matrix.indiagri.in/room-heaters-report)
- An early cold spell drove winter-wear sales (mid-Oct to mid-Nov sales >2x prior year on Snapdeal, 2020) — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/early-cold-spell-pushes-winter-wear-sales-trends-show-177138)
- Appliance makers ramp up heating-product production ahead of winter, expecting robust growth — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/appliance-makers-expect-robust-growth-from-heating-products-this-winter-news-244046)
- Lint removers and hot-water heat pads are routinely listed as Indian winter must-haves; lint removers retail Rs 300–1,500 — [Instacuppa guide](https://instacuppastore.com/blogs/main-page/lint-remover-complete-guide-for-indian-wardrobes-2026); [How2Shout (2019)](https://www.how2shout.com/?p=44453)
- Flipkart listing example: electric gel hot water bag Rs 245 vs Rs 999 MRP, marketed "for winter" — [Flipkart](https://www.flipkart.com/rbs-electric-hot-water-with-filled-gel-bag-pain-relief-gel-pack/p/itmb3d1699b73d18)

### Inferences
- Best winter window for ads: launch early-to-mid Nov (pre-cold-wave), peak mid-Dec to mid-Jan, kill by early Feb. Geo-target North India (Delhi NCR, Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan, Bihar, J&K, HP, Uttarakhand); South/coastal demand is weak.
- Strongest dropship fits: low-cost, light, non-sizing items — electric hot water bags, plug-in mini room heaters, lint removers (woollens), USB/mini humidifiers, heated/insulated lunch boxes, cracked-heel/moisturizing socks, winter skincare (lip balm, body cream), touch-screen gloves, anti-fog car film/spray (fog season Dec–Jan in North India).
- Heated insoles and battery hand warmers likely niche in India (low awareness, high price); not stocked by DeoDap either.

## 3. COD dropship economics on Meta in India (CPM/CPC/CPA, RTO, shipping, markup, breakeven ROAS)

### Takeaway
Indian Meta CPMs are cheap (roughly Rs 50–200 typical, rising ~20–30% in festive peaks), but COD RTO of ~20–30% (worse in tier-2/3 and in the Rs 500–1,000 AOV band) erodes reported ROAS by ~30%+. At a ~3x price/landed-cost markup, breakeven ROAS on Meta-reported revenue is ~2.5–3x; at 2x markup it becomes ~5x, which is rarely achievable.

### Cited Findings
**Ad costs**
- India Meta average CPM Rs 50–120; CPC Rs 8–25; Reels CPC Rs 8–18, IG feed Rs 14–28, FB feed Rs 12–25; e-commerce CTR ~1.3%; ROAS: supplements 4.5x, fashion 3.8x, home decor 3.5x, electronics 2.5x (2025 client data) — [OwlClaw](https://owlclaw.com/benchmarks/meta-ads-benchmarks-india)
- Alternative CPM figures: Rs 80–200 for dropshipping (vs $5–15 US) — [Dropshipping Calculator blog](https://dropshipping-calculator-six.vercel.app/blog/facebook-ads-roas-dropshipping-india-guide); Rs 120–350 FB CPM — [Steemit agency guide](https://steemit.steemapps.com/metaads/@vidhanchavan/meta-ads-services-in-pune-complete-guide-to-facebook-and-instagram-ads-cost-in-india-2026); one advertiser reports open-targeting CPM as low as Rs 6–7 — [Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/metas-ad-prices-rise-14-globally-in-q3-just-1-in-apac-the-india-equation-159408.html). Sources conflict substantially.
- Meta ad prices rose 14% globally in Q3 but only ~1% in APAC — [Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/metas-ad-prices-rise-14-globally-in-q3-just-1-in-apac-the-india-equation-159408.html)
- Festive CPM inflation: "CPMs up 30% across platforms" (Oct 2025 headline) — [Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/festive-season-news/festive-rush-d2c-brands-double-spends-on-regional-markets-cpms-up-30-across-platforms-148281.html); Meta CPM +20–25% in festivity (undated) — [Drim](https://drim.one/blog/article/festive-season-marketing-campaigns-10)
- Meta ROAS for Indian D2C in 2026: 1.8x–4.2x by category/stage; reported 2.5x ROAS can be ~1.6x net of COD returns/RTO — [upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/how-to-improve-roas-d2c-india-2026/) (via search summary)
- Dropship AOV in India typically Rs 399–999; target ROAS 2.5–4x for ~35% margin stores; scale only 30–40% above breakeven; native-style video performs 2–3x better; retargeting 3–4x better ROAS than cold — [Dropshipping Calculator blog](https://dropshipping-calculator-six.vercel.app/blog/facebook-ads-roas-dropshipping-india-guide)
- Breakeven ROAS formula: Revenue ÷ (Revenue − non-ad costs). Example: Rs 499 price, Rs 215 product+shipping+COD → 1.76x (before RTO) — [Dropshipping Calculator blog](https://dropshipping-calculator-six.vercel.app/blog/facebook-ads-roas-dropshipping-india-guide)
- No reliable India-specific cost-per-purchase benchmark for dropshipping found.

**RTO**

- GoKwik 2026: average COD RTO 20–25%; national RTO 20–30%; COD = 58–64% of tier-2/3 orders but 76–83% of RTO volume — [base.com](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/?p=22444)
- Shipway ShipNotes: overall COD RTO ~26% (via search summary) — [base.com benchmarks](https://www.base.com/en-IN/blog/india-ecommerce-benchmarks-2026-how-brands-reduce-rto-in-india-and-hit-accuracy-sla-targets)

**Shipping / COD fees**
- COD fee typically Rs 25–50 flat or 1.5–2.5% of order value (whichever higher) + 18% GST — [ShipPrime](https://shipprime.live/resources/blogs/how-to-calculate-shipping-costs-india)
- Shiprocket illustrative COD fixed charge Rs 36; rates depend on courier, zone, volumetric weight — [Shiprocket support](https://support.shiprocket.in/support/solutions/articles/152000000168-how-to-check-the-applied-cod-charges-)
- Effective per-order logistics cost 15–30% higher once RTO/reverse included — [ShipPrime](https://shipprime.live/resources/blogs/cod-vs-prepaid-rto-profitability)
- Anecdotal seller complaints of weight disputes and COD charges rising — [Shopify app reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/1644777)
- No verified 2025–26 Shiprocket 500g rate card found.

**Markup**
- No source confirms a "3x landed cost" rule; Factori example: Rs 1,499 sale vs Rs 700 supplier cost (~2.1x) and argues logistics/competition erode margin — [Factori](https://blog.factori.com/2025/11/10/is-dropshipping-profitable-in-india-in-2025)
- Claims ~60% of Indian dropshipping stores fail at scale — [CIIM](https://www.ciim.in/why-60-of-dropshipping-stores-fail-at-scale-in-india) (unverified)

### Inferences (worked model — my calculation, assumptions labelled)
Assumptions: forward shipping Rs 80 (0.5–1 kg, all shipped orders), COD fee Rs 40 (delivered only), RTO return freight Rs 80, RTO 25%, returned product restocked by supplier (no product cost on RTO), no GST/PG/packaging.
- Product cost Rs 349 (DeoDap 400W wall heater), sell Rs 999 (~2.9x): per shipped order revenue 0.75×999 = 749; costs 262 + 80 + 30 + 20 = 392; contribution Rs 357 → breakeven CPA ~Rs 357 per Meta "purchase"; breakeven ROAS on Meta-reported revenue ≈ 999/357 ≈ 2.8x.
- Same product at Rs 699 (2x): revenue 524, costs 392 → contribution Rs 132 → breakeven ROAS ≈ 5.3x (practically unviable).
- Product cost Rs 175 (electric lint remover) at Rs 599 (3.4x): revenue 449; costs 131 + 80 + 30 + 20 = 261 → contribution 188 → breakeven ROAS ≈ 3.2x. Low-ticket items suffer because fixed shipping/RTO costs dominate.
- Implication: target selling price ≥3x landed cost AND AOV ≥ ~Rs 799 (or bundles/combos) so fixed logistics is ≤ ~15% of price; avoid the Rs 500–1,000 single-item band where RTO is highest unless using partial-COD/prepaid incentives.
- During peak (late Nov–Dec), budget for ~20–30% higher CPMs; the winter window is short, so test in early Nov.
- Levers: partial COD (Rs 49–99 advance), prepaid discount, WhatsApp/IVR order confirmation, pincode blocking of high-RTO areas.

## 4. Categories with high RTO/return risk to avoid

### Takeaway
Avoid size-dependent apparel/footwear/ethnic wear (25–45% RTO), and be careful with gadgets that have defect/expectation risk and fragile glass items; low-RTO categories are beauty/personal care, home care, jewellery/accessories (non-sized).

### Cited Findings
- Category RTO (2026, base.com, mostly unsourced): Ethnic wear 30–45%; Fashion/apparel 25–40%; Footwear 20–35%; Sports/activewear 15–28%; Home furnishings 10–25%; Electronics/gadgets 8–18%; Pet care 8–18%; Supplements 6–14%; Beauty & personal care 5–15%; Home care/cleaning 5–12% (transit damage driver); Jewellery & accessories 4–12% — [base.com](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/?p=22444)
- Conflict: electronics RTO cited up to 40% by another source vs 8–18% above — [Edesy calculator](https://edesy.in/tools/cod-rto-calculator); fashion/footwear/general merchandise "touching 40%" — [ShipPrime](https://shipprime.live/resources/blogs/cod-vs-prepaid-rto-profitability)
- 63% of online shoppers reportedly practise bracketing (ordering multiple sizes) — [base.com](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/?p=22444)

### Inferences
- Avoid: sized winter apparel (jackets, thermals, sized gloves), heated insoles (size + battery), fragile glass kettles, high-wattage heaters with fire/defect complaints (higher-ticket, refusal risk), cheap electronics prone to DOA.
- Prefer: hot water bags, lint removers, humidifiers, organizers, makeup brush sets, LED decor (check packaging for breakage), skincare/foot care.
- Safety/compliance note: room heaters and electrical appliances may fall under BIS QCO mandatory certification — verify supplier BIS marking before advertising (not researched here).

### Gaps
- Category RTO data for "winter gadgets" specifically not available.
- BIS QCO status of plug-in heaters/kettles not researched.
