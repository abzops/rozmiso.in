# Dropshipping in India, from the inside

Rozmiso research · October 2026 · Interactive version: https://claude.ai/artifact/FjAzo53cyXySZ5u1eWHFyz

> Educational, not legal or tax advice. GST rates changed on 22 Sept 2025 and the Income-tax Act was replaced from April 2026. Confirm your setup with a CA.

## 1. How it works
- Three parties: **customer → you (store and seller of record) → supplier** (holds stock and ships under your name).
- You own: the brand, marketing and ad spend, the customer, invoices and refunds, legal liability, and cash flow (you pay the supplier before COD money arrives).
- You don't control: stock, product quality, packing speed, courier, or how many others sell the same product.
- **Dropshipping removes inventory risk and adds marketing risk.** In India, COD and RTO (returned parcels) decide whether you make money.

## 2. Worked example: DeoDap (dropshipping.deodap.com)
- Rajkot wholesaler, founded 2014, about 400 staff. Claims 12k+ SKUs in 35+ categories, 19k+ pin codes, 24–72h delivery, COD, white-label shipping, GST billing, 15k+ sellers.
- **Order flow:** Instagram ad → your Shopify store → COD confirmation by WhatsApp → order pushed to DeoDap panel → you pay wholesale price + shipping from your wallet → DeoDap packs (white label) → courier delivers or the parcel returns (RTO) → courier sends COD money to you about a week after delivery → you handle invoice, support and returns.
- Goods and money move in opposite directions. You pay first and get paid later.
- **How DeoDap earns:** about 10 paid plans (only the Mobile App plan price, ₹5,900, is published; the rest need a callback), plus margin in the "wholesale" price, plus services (account management, ads, listings, trademarks). The platform earns whether or not you profit.
- **Read the site critically:** countdown timers and a "webinar running now" banner (sales funnel); "₹0 risk" covers stock only; hidden prices; the calculator still offers 12% and 28% GST slabs (removed in GST 2.0).
- **The calculator has two answers:** "₹181 profit/order" but "₹25,608/month". 181 × 300 − 5,000 = 49,300, not 25,608. The site's own JS comments call the ₹181 per-order card "optimistic" and the monthly figure "honest".
- **Ask before paying:** the exact plan price and refund terms; RTO product credit and return freight; whose name is on the invoice; dispatch SLA and out-of-stock handling; stock sync method; BIS/FSSAI certificates; damage claim process; samples and seller references.

## 3. The real numbers (300 orders/month, ₹799 product, 18% GST)
| Scenario | Key assumptions | Monthly profit |
|---|---|---|
| DeoDap default | Ads ₹120/order, RTO 20%, cancel 10%, no return freight/COD fee/gateway/returns | **+₹25,608** |
| Realistic new store | Ads ₹250, RTO 30%, return freight charged, 70% COD at ₹35 fee, 2% gateway, 5% returns | **≈ −₹41,900** |
| Well-run store (₹1,199 price, cost ₹300) | Ads ₹220, RTO 14%, cancel 6%, 45% COD | **≈ +₹58,200** |

- The three inputs that decide the result: **ad cost per order, RTO %, COD share**.
- Benchmarks: COD RTO is commonly 25–30%, up to 40% in fashion and general merchandise; prepaid is under 2%; the Nov 2025 festive peak reached about 39%. The ad cost range of ₹200–400+ for new stores is an assumption; measure your own.
- A ₹799 item rarely survives paid ads plus COD. Aim for ₹999–1,999 order value through pricing, bundles and upsells.

## 4. Choosing a supplier
| Type | Examples (verify current status) | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| B2B catalogue + paid plans | DeoDap, Baapstore | Plan fees, shared catalogue, markup |
| Shopify dropship apps | Roposo Clout and others | App fees, recent reviews |
| Print-on-demand | Qikink | Margins, print quality |
| Reseller marketplace | Meesho | Low prices; you don't own the customer |
| Manufacturers / wholesalers | IndiaMART, Surat/Rajkot/Delhi clusters | Shipping, minimum orders |
| Own stock + 3PL (next step) | Proven SKUs only | Inventory risk |

Due diligence: order samples shipped to another state; check the supplier's GSTIN; get a written agreement (SLA, RTO, damage, invoice format, price notice); keep a backup supplier; no branded or "first copy" goods.

## 5. Legal and compliance
1. **Entity:** a sole proprietorship (PAN + current account) is cheapest; an LLP or Pvt Ltd limits liability. Udyam registration is optional.
2. **GST:** inter-state sales need **registration regardless of turnover** (Sec 24 CGST). The marketplace exemption (Notification 34/2023, turnover under ₹40L) covers **intra-state sales only**. Slabs are now mostly 5% / 18% (40% for luxury and sin goods). The supplier invoices you; you invoice the customer; you claim input credit. Marketplaces deduct **GST TCS of 0.5%** and **TDS of 0.1%** (Sec 194-O, now Sec 393(1) of the new Income-tax Act). Both can be claimed back.
3. **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020:** seller details, grievance officer, return and refund policies, country of origin, all-inclusive price. **Dark patterns are banned** (CCPA 2023), including fake timers and fake reviews.
4. **Legal Metrology:** MRP, net quantity, manufacturer/packer/importer details, month of manufacture, and customer care contact, on both the package and the listing.
5. **BIS QCOs** (toys, many electronics, helmets, cookware and more; the list changes). **FSSAI** for food and supplements; **CDSCO** for cosmetics and medical devices. Avoid these categories at first.
6. **Trademark:** search first, then file in Class 35 plus your product class. Don't use other brands' names or images.
7. **DPDP Act 2023 + Rules (notified 13 Nov 2025):** most obligations apply from **13 May 2027**. Have a privacy notice and get consent for marketing.
8. **Platform rules:** Amazon allows dropshipping only if you're the **seller of record**, with no other seller named on the slip, invoice or package. Payment gateways need KYC and policy pages. Meta bans misleading ads.

## 6. Daily operations
Channels: own Shopify site (most control, needs ad skill), Amazon/Flipkart (strict rules, fees), Meesho (thin margins), WhatsApp/Instagram reselling (cheap to test).

Daily loop: sync stock → check ad spend against orders → **confirm COD orders** → push to supplier and top up wallet → work NDRs within 24h → support. Weekly: reconcile COD, invoices and ad spend; remove products that lose money.

To reduce RTO: prepaid discount, small COD advance, block repeat refusers, honest product photos and videos, dispatch within 24h.

## 7. Shipping and COD
- Rates rise by zone (local, regional, metro, rest of India, North-East/J&K). "24–72h" applies to metros; remote areas take 5–8 days.
- You're billed on actual weight or volumetric weight (L×B×H/5000), whichever is higher.
- COD remittance: about D+8 days (Shiprocket, paid 3 times a week). Early payout costs about 0.5–1%.
- RTO usually adds return freight. Confirm whether the product cost is credited back to you.
- On shared catalogues, white-label packing is a plain box. Branded packaging needs your own stock.
- **Rozmiso / Stack n Stock angle:** test products by dropshipping, then move proven SKUs into stock you control. Speed, packaging, quality control and RTO handling are where warehouse capability turns into margin.

## 8. What nobody tells you
Shared catalogues lead to price wars · selling items that are out of stock · quality you haven't checked · cash squeeze as you grow · ad account bans · marketplace fund holds · GST notices · money spent on courses and plans with no sales · depending on one supplier.

## 9. A 90-day start plan
- **Days 1–30:** pick a niche, shortlist 15–20 products, order samples from 2–3 suppliers, register entity and GST, run a trademark search, build a compliant store, set up payment gateway and COD confirmation.
- **Days 31–60:** test 5–8 products at ₹500–1,000/day each; track ad cost/order, confirmation %, RTO % and profit per delivered order daily; drop products with no profit after about ₹5k ad spend.
- **Days 61–90:** scale 1–2 winners, raise order value, push prepaid (target RTO under 15%). Only then consider paid plans. Plan your own stock for the winners.
