# Rozmiso marketing and operations

Prepared materials. No advertisements, creator messages or customer campaigns were sent or purchased.

## Audience hypotheses

Start with garment care: people who want to maintain clothing they already own. Test a separate upholstery-care angle only if the inspected device and instructions support it. These are hypotheses, not established targeting results. Avoid spending across four unrelated products before validating one.

## Three video scripts

Film a real sample, not AI-generated evidence of product performance. Use consistent lighting and the same fabric before/after. Record the actual time taken; avoid hidden edits that exaggerate the result. Keep all care instructions visible in the description. Publish sales calls to action only after ordering and delivery work.

### 1. A favourite item
- 0–3 seconds: close-up of visible surface pilling on a suitable garment. Caption: “A favourite piece, ready for a little care.”
- 3–12 seconds: show the actual test and device use, following instructions.
- 12–18 seconds: same area under the same light. Narration describes only the change you observed.
- 18–22 seconds: product and included items. CTA: “Explore the details at Rozmiso.”

### 2. What is in the box?
- 0–4 seconds: unopened sample package.
- 4–12 seconds: lay out the exact contents and label them correctly.
- 12–18 seconds: show charging instructions and controls without guessing runtime or charger requirements.
- 18–22 seconds: “Check the care guidance and current availability at Rozmiso.”

### 3. Before you use it
- 0–4 seconds: show garment care label and a hidden test area.
- 4–12 seconds: demonstrate a light touch on suitable fabric.
- 12–18 seconds: device off, routine cleaning according to instructions.
- 18–22 seconds: “A small tool needs the right care. Read the details before choosing.”

Suggested caption: “A little care for the pieces you already love. See product details, care guidance and current availability at Rozmiso. Results vary with fabric and condition.”

## Test plan

Before paid traffic: sample accepted, supplier agreement, confirmed costs/stock, payment enabled, policies complete and fulfillment/order test passed. Install the official Meta channel only with the owner's account/permission approval. Configure consent/data sharing deliberately. Verify product view, add-to-cart, checkout and purchase events, comparing one purchase against one Shopify order.

Set a total spending cap after the owner provides it. There is no approved budget yet. Choose a few distinct creatives and one product destination. Compare actual acquisition cost and settled contribution against the updated economics. Do not scale based on impressions, likes or booked revenue alone.

## Order routine

Daily: verify payment status and valid order details, check real stock, hand off to the agreed supplier, record dispatch/tracking, respond to genuine enquiries and monitor delivery exceptions. Confirm COD orders through the agreed workflow before dispatch; do not collect unnecessary identity data.

Weekly: reconcile provider payouts, COD remittances, supplier invoices, freight/RTO, refunds, subscription charges and advertising. Update the same order-date cohort in the tracker until it is settled. Keep pending cohorts pending; only close after the return/settlement cycle is reconciled. Record authentic feedback and recurring defects.

## Customer response drafts

Availability: “Thanks for asking about [PRODUCT]. It is currently unavailable for orders. We will confirm stock and delivery details before asking you to pay.”

Product question: “Thanks for your question about [PRODUCT]. Here is the information confirmed for this model: [VERIFIED ANSWER]. Please follow the included care instructions.”

Delivery issue: “Thank you for your order number. We are checking shipment [TRACKING] and will update you by [REALISTIC FOLLOW-UP].”

Damaged item: “I’m sorry there is an issue. Please share your order number and photos of the item/package where available. We will review them under our published policy and confirm the next step.”

Do not promise a reply deadline, refund, warranty or delivery date you cannot fulfill.
